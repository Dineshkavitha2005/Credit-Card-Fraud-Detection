Financial Fraud Typologies Detected by Sentinel

Modern cybercriminals exploit multi-layered vectors to test stolen credentials and drain balances. Sentinel actively classifies and intercepts complex fraud patterns across multiple operational dimensions.

1. Distributed Card Testing (BIN Attacks)

Attackers utilize automated botnets to test thousands of stolen Bank Identification Numbers (BINs) through micro-transactions ($0.50 to $2.00) on e-commerce merchants. Sentinel detects card testing through:

  • Abnormal frequencies of low-value transactions targeting identical merchant categories.
  • High authorization failure rates originating from clustered IP subnets.
  • Instant rate limiting and autonomous IP blocking to protect merchant authorization quotas.

2. High-Velocity Payment Bursts

When fraudsters validate a working credit card, they attempt to rapidly monetize the account before the genuine cardholder notices. Sentinel tracks rolling transaction counters per card and per identity:

  • Flags multiple transactions executed within seconds or minutes of each other.
  • Detects escalating dollar amounts indicative of progressive balance testing.
  • Enforces configurable velocity rules to temporarily freeze payment attempts.

3. Geographical Anomalies & Impossible Travel

Physical and IP geolocation discrepancies provide strong indicators of account compromise. Sentinel analyzes geographic telemetry:

  • Detects transactions originating from different countries or regions within physically impossible time frames (e.g. New York at 10:00 AM and London at 10:45 AM).
  • Identifies suspicious data center IPs, known VPN gateways, and residential proxy networks.
  • Correlates card issuing bank location with transaction terminal location.

4. Nocturnal Activity & Temporal Deviations

Fraud rings frequently operate during off-peak hours (midnight to 5:00 AM cardholder local time) to delay discovery and prevent two-factor authentication alerts from being noticed immediately. Sentinel evaluates temporal patterns:

  • Applies dynamic risk weight multipliers to off-hour high-value transactions.
  • Compares current transaction timing against historical cardholder habit baselines.
  • Requires supervisory analyst sign-off for high-value off-hours payment releases.

5. Multi-Card Account Takeover (ATO)

In credential stuffing and checkout hijacking campaigns, a single malicious device or session attempts purchases using multiple distinct card numbers. Sentinel monitors cross-card correlations:

  • Clusters transactions sharing device fingerprints, user agents, or IP addresses.
  • Flags accounts adding and cycling through three or more payment cards within an hour.
  • Automatically blacklists identified card numbers to prevent cascade losses.
